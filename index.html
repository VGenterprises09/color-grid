📖 The Legend of Vagoon Gadupiti

Chapter I — Origin

In the winter of 2019, inside a glass-walled conference room in Hyderabad, Telangana, a mandatory four-hour "synergy alignment" meeting was underway. Everyone was present. Nobody was listening. In the back row sat Vagoon Gadupiti, Senior Software Engineer, color enthusiast, and — as of that afternoon — the accidental inventor of the world's greatest grid-flooding puzzle game.

"I just started drawing colored squares on my notepad," Vagoon later recalled. "Then I thought, what if you could flood-fill the whole thing? By the time the meeting ended, I had the rules, the algorithm, and a strong opinion about which six colors to use." He filed no patents. He told no one. He simply built it — and thus, the legend began.

Chapter II — The Rivalry

For months, Vagoon played alone, quietly perfecting his craft. Then he made the mistake of showing the game to Pradeep Naidu, his colleague and self-declared "puzzle guy." Three days later, Pradeep beat Vagoon's Hard mode record by two moves.

Vagoon did not speak to Pradeep for eleven days. He then spent those eleven days optimizing his strategy. He reclaimed the record. Pradeep disputes the score to this day. Vagoon does not acknowledge the dispute. "Pradeep cheated," Vagoon says, calmly. "He used Easy mode psychology on a Hard mode grid. That is not how Vagoon plays."

Chapter III — The Philosophy

Vagoon's approach to the game mirrors his approach to life: deliberate, patient, and absolutely convinced that saffron is the correct color to start with. "A good flood-fill is like a good biryani," he once explained to his cousin Suresh at a family function. "You cannot rush the dum. You cannot rush the flood. You commit to the color, and you trust the process."

His mother, Lakshmi Gadupiti, remains supportive but confused. "It is that color block thing on his phone," she says proudly. "He is very good at it." She is not wrong.
